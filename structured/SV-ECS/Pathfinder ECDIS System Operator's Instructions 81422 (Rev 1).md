### Warnings and cautions

Note: The warnings and cautions detailed in this document apply to the operation of the equipment. For warnings and cautions related to installation and maintenance, please refer to the relevant installation instructions. For a list of applicable documents, refer to: p.15 — Product documentation

### Aid to navigation

Unless specifically stated as “non-type approved”, the equipment detailed in this document comply with relevant SOLAS regulations and are provided as an aid to navigation. The equipment should be used in accordance with the SOLAS regulations.

### Safety warnings

##### Warning: Fire Risk

Equipment may contain materials which produce toxic fumes if burnt.

##### Warning: Product operation

This product must be operated in accordance with the operation instructions provided. Failure to do so could result in personal injury, damage to your vessel and/or poor product performance.

##### Warning: Operational latitudes

The maximum North and South latitudes at which the system is qualified for use is 85°. The system should not be used for navigation above this latitude.

##### Warning: Day mode brightness warning

Switching from Night mode to Day mode instantly increases the display brightness to maximum. This will impact the operator’s night vision, due to the relative brightness of Day mode in night time conditions.

### Product warnings

##### Warning: Anti-virus protection

This product does not include protection against computer viruses. Before inserting any memory device, ensure it is free from computer viruses by scanning the device with a suitable anti-virus application with up to date virus definitions.

##### Warning: Sun cover contains magnets

The display’s sun cover contains magnets which may interfere with magnetic sensitive devices (such as compasses, attitude sensors and gyroscopes). To prevent potential interference: • The display must be installed a safe distance away from magnetic sensitive devices. • When the display is in use the sun cover must be stored away from such devices.

### 3.1 Pathfinder ECDIS overview

The Raymarine Commercial Pathfinder ECDIS comprises of a display with an integrated processor, a Data Collection Unit (DCU), an Internet gateway and display peripherals. The Raymarine Commercial Pathfinder ECDIS complies with IMO (International Maritime Organization) standards and is Type Approved to the ECDIS standard IEC 61174 edition 4. Pathfinder ECDIS is built on the Nautilus ECDIS Kernel, ensuring future S-100 support, reliable hardware and a software roadmap to S-100 compliance.

### 3.5 eSyncBox overview

- The eSyncBox (part number: CW-50620) is a cyber security data gateway that can be used to connect to the ChartWorld eSync service.

The eSyncBox is IEC 61162–460 compliant and uses a secure VPN tunnel so that data can be exchanged safely between the eSyncBox and the NavCloud ChartWorld server. eSync eliminates the need for AVCS (Admiralty Vectors Chart Service) data stored on physical media onboard vessels. eSync protects and secures the network using a multi-layer inspection firewall, network and transport layer access control (based on addresses, ports, and protocols) and enables download, installation and storage of weekly AVCS Base Media, exchange of MyRA routes and CIO+ data files between the system and the NavCloud ChartWorld servers. The eSyncBox offers a wired Ethernet WAN port, Cellular (4G) and Wi-Fi connections which can be used to connect directly or in-directly to the Internet.

Target importance is determined by the [CPA] , and [TCPA] values configured within the [Target Settings] menu.

Note: Using the [Maximum Range] and [Maximum Speed] slider settings has no effect on the capacity warnings.

##### AIS targets

The system can process up to 6,000 AIS targets and display up to 500 AIS targets simultaneously as sleeping or activated targets, based on user set filters. AIS targets can be activated by manual selection. A benefit of AIS is that AIS targets are visible in adverse weather conditions and provide an earlier indication of a target movement.

Note:
- Not all surface objects/vessels may be equipped with AIS.
- The accuracy of AIS data is dependent on the accuracy of each ships navigation systems.
- AIS equipped vessels can disable transmission of AIS data.

The AIS system has two major classifications, Class A for large ships providing a full set of data and Class B for small craft, which provide a subset of data. AIS compliments the target tracking feature to support radar in an anti-collision role. While the user functionality of Radar target tracking and AIS is very similar, there are important differences. Radar target tracking systems are subject to error sources that do not apply to AIS, for example: • Low Signal-to-Noise ratio - Targets may appear to fade on the screen. The target track may indicate a weak target alert, and in extreme cases, lose the target. Other echoes or clutter appearing in the tacking window may result in target swap or cause vector instability. • Side Lobes/Reflections - Ownship structures within the radar antenna beam or presence of other large targets, can generate false targets or elongated targets. This may result in tracking errors and could give false indications of CPA. Reference targets affected by reflections may become unreliable and it is advised that multiple reference targets are used to reduce error.

- Ownship pitch and yaw.

### 7.11 Contour settings

The [Contour settings] are used to differentiate between safe (navigable) waters and unsafe (non-navigable) waters. The specified contour values are used to create lines that are drawn on the cartography which connect points of equal depth creating a visualization of bottom structure. These contours use color fills to indicate depth in relation to each other.

The depths at which these contours are drawn can be customized from the [Contour settings] located in the [Chart display settings] menu. The following contour settings are available: 1.   [Shallow Contour] — Depths from zero to the specified [Shallow Contour] depth will be within the [Shallow Contour]. 2. [Safety Depth] — The [Safety Depth] value is used to identify isolated areas within the [Shallow Contour] which may be safe to navigate. 3.   [Safety Contour] — Depths from zero to the specified [Safety Contour] depth will be within the [Safety Contour]. The [Safety Contour] identifies waters that are unsafe for navigation.

### 7.12 CPA and TCPA

CPA (Closest Point of Approach) and TCPA (Time to Closest Point of Approach) are used to identify targets which are dangerous because they are a potential collision threat. AIS and ARPA targets become dangerous if they will cross your path within the distance and time specified in the [CPA] and [TCPA] settings. When a target is, or becomes dangerous the [Dangerous target] alarm (collision warning alarm) is triggered. The Dangerous target alarm can only be turned off if the target is no longer being tracked or the alarm condition is no longer present (i.e.: the target is no longer dangerous). [CPA] and [TCPA] settings are located in the [Target settings] menu, which is accessed by selecting the [TGT] icon from the [Toolbar] .

- Selecting the current value will open the onscreen numeric keypad so that the current value can be adjusted.
- [CPA] can be adjusted from 0 NM to 6 NM (Default value is 2 NM).
- [TCPA] can be adjusted from 0 mins to 60 mins (Default value is 12 mins).

### 8.2 Lines Of Position (LOPs)

LOPs are manually plotted bearing and range lines that can be used to calculate ownship position. By plotting lines between your position and other objects it is possible to plot your own position. 2 or more LOPs can be used to plot a manual position fix for your vessel. The more LOPs that are used to create a position fix the more accurate the fix should be. Multiple position fixes can then be used to track your movement over time. There are different methods of determining position using LOPs: Bearing LOPs: A hand held compass, the ship’s compass or a radar scanner can be used to determine the bearing of a fixed object on the chart an LOP can then be created at that bearing and placed directly over the object. Ownship should be located at some point along that LOP. For higher level of accuracy a second LOP can be created for another fixed object, ownship position should be at the intersect point between the 2 LOPs.

If the LOPs do not overlap subsequent LOPs can be placed over additional objects to identify ownship position. Other bearing LOP methods:

- Transit method — When 2 objects in view are in direct alignment an LOP can be created at that bearing. when the LOP passes directly over both objects, ownship should be located at some point along that LOP.
- Depth contour method: — If you have accurate depth transducer readings you can use a fixed object to create an LOP at that bearing. when the LOP is placed directly over the object you can compare the depth contours under the LOP against your current depth transducer reading, ownship position should be at a point along the LOP where the depth contour matches the current depth reading.
- Double the angle off the bow — Bearing LOPs can be used to determine distance from an object using the “double the angle off the bow” method. Check and create a bearing LOP and place over the object. continue travel until the bearing to the object is double the first bearing (e.g.: if the original bearing was 45° create another bearing LOP at 90°. Using d = s x t (distance equals speed x time) you can work out the distance to the object (e.g.: if it took 25 minutes (0.416 hours) to travel between the 2 points at 10 knots the calculation would be (10 x 0.416 = 4.16 NM) so the object is 4.16 NM away. Range LOPs: When the distance from a fixed, charted object is known, a distance LOP can be created directly over the fixed object, ownship should be located at some point along the circle. For a higher level of accuracy a second distance LOP can be created over another fixed object, ownship should be located at the point that the circles intersect. Using range LOPs may cause there to be 2 intersect points, in this case further range LOPs can be created to provide a higher position accuracy.

##### Position fix calculations

The calculation the system uses for determining a Position fix is implemented as an iterative process to define the smallest possible error. A geometric solution would estimate the fix position within a triangle created by the geometry of the plotted LOPs. The given solution reflects that nearby objects will have a greater impact on the calculation and therefore the position fix will be influenced by bearing lines of closer/nearby objects. Even though the calculated fix may be unexpected, it will be more plausible than the geometric result.

### 8.17 Target Association

Target association allows association (linking) of ARPA targets with AIS targets that appear to represent the same vessel.

###### The Target association feature is only available when both AIS and ARPA

targets are enabled. [Target Association] is enable using the [Target Association] toggle switch located in the [Target Settings] menu. When [Target Association] is enabled, the system will compare the Bearing, Range, COG and SOG of AIS and ARPA targets to determine if they are associated (i.e.: the same target), based on the [Delta] setting.

The settings for [Target Association] are accessed by selecting the [TA Settings] tab from the [Target Settings] menu. Use the [Priority] drop down box to determine whether AIS or ARPA target data has priority when targets are “associated”. Priority

1. [Target Association] disabled — When [Target Association] is disabled ARPA and AIS targets which may represent the same vessel will appear separately.
2. Priority: AIS — When AIS is set as the [Priority], the displayed target’s information will be based on AIS data. The AIS target symbol will be used and will include a thinner circle (ARPA symbol) drawn around the triangle.
3. Priority: ARPA — When ARPA is set as the [Priority], the displayed target’s information will be based on ARPA data. The ARPA target symbol will be used and will include a thinner triangle (AIS symbol) drawn around the circle.

###### Delta

The [Delta] values are the values which determine whether the system treats the targets as “associated”. There are 3 [Delta] value settings • 50% — Bearing: 0.4°, Range: 45 m, COG: 5.0° & SOG: 1.3 kn. • 100% (default) — — Bearing: 0.8°, Range: 90 m, COG: 10.0° & SOG: 2.6 kn.

- Best cleaning practices.
- When cleaning products:
- Switch off power supply.
- Use a clean damp cloth to wipe clean.
- Do NOT use: abrasive, acidic, ammonia, solvent or other chemical-based cleaning products.
- Do NOT use a jet wash.

##### Cleaning the display case

- The display is a sealed unit and does not require regular cleaning. If it is necessary to clean the display, follow this basic procedure:
1. Switch off the power to the display.
2. Wipe the case with a clean, lint-free cloth.
3. If necessary, use a mild detergent to remove grease marks.

##### Cleaning the display screen

- A coating is applied to the display screen. This makes it water repellent, and prevents glare. To avoid damaging this coating, follow this procedure:
1. Switch off the power to the display.
2. Rinse the screen with fresh water to remove all dirt particles and salt deposits.
3. Allow the screen to dry naturally.
4. If any smears remain, very gently wipe the screen with a clean microfibre cleaning cloth.
